What siteread sends, and what it never does

Your log holds your visitors' IP addresses and every URL on your site. None of that leaves your machine. The one thing that can leave is a week of totals, and only when you say yes.

check, digest

On your machine

Nothing is sent except plain GETs of the vendors' public IP lists, with the user-agent siteread/{{ version }}. There is no reverse-DNS check either: a PTR lookup would send your visitor's IP to your resolver.

share

Only on a yes

It prints every byte it would send, then asks. --dry-run prints and stops. What it sends is exactly what it printed.

the feed

Public data, opt-in

One file with every vendor's IP list, for anyone who would rather make one request than nine. Off unless you pass feedUrl; nothing is kept about who fetched it.

A whole share, byte for byte

From the test site. Thirteen numbers for the last 7 days, a week label, the version, and a random id.

{{ payload }}

Never sent

  • hostnames, URLs or paths
  • visitor IP addresses
  • user-agents and referrers
  • search query text
  • your goal names

The payload is built from a fixed list of numbers, and the service refuses any field it does not know rather than dropping it.

What the service keeps

The share as sent, plus the day it arrived (not the time). Not your IP address: the code never reads it, and api.siteread.dev, where shares and the feed go, keeps no log.

What gets published

Per number, the 25th, 50th and 75th percentile once 10 anonymous sites report it, and how many sites shared each week. Never a single site's row, never an id. synacl.com is named because its owner chose that.

Your id

128 random bits in .siteread/share-id, made the first time you share. It lets the benchmark count sites instead of shares, and a second share of the same week replaces the first. Delete the file to start over as a new site.

This site

No cookies, no analytics, no third-party requests: the fonts are served from siteread.dev itself.

Like the sites siteread is made for, siteread.dev keeps an ordinary access log of its pages: each request's address, time, page, referrer and user-agent. Only root can read it on the server. Once a week its owner copies it to their own machine and runs siteread on it, to see who read this site. Every line is deleted after 28 days, in both places. It is never shared or sent anywhere else, and api.siteread.dev keeps no log at all.
